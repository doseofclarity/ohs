Occupational Health & Safety Nursing

“Promotion, protection, and restoration of workers’ health”

Definition & Scope of Occupational Health Nursing

  • Occupational Health Nursing – specialty practice that provides and delivers health care services to workers and worker populations.
  • Focus: promotion, protection, and restoration of workers’ health within a safe work environment.
  • Autonomous practice requiring independent decisions and creative solutions.
  • Integrates public health theory and principles.

Public health perspective:

Workers spend 1/4 to 1/3 of their lives at work. The PHN can act as an occupational health nurse to assess health needs of the working population and design healthful interventions.

Functions of Occupational Health Nurse (PD 856, Sanitation Code)

  • Lead sanitary & industrial hygiene inspections in workplaces.
  • Recommend issuance/suspension/revocation of business permits based on sanitation compliance.
  • Coordinate with government agencies on IRR implementation.
  • Attend complaints related to industrial hygiene; recommend corrective measures.
  • Provide, install, and maintain control facilities and protective barriers.
  • Inform workers about hazards and reasons for control measures.
  • Conduct periodic health examinations related to worker exposure.
  • Implement control measures to reduce noise, dust, and other hazards.
  • Ensure proper use and maintenance of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).
  • Provide occupational health services and facilities for employees.
  • Elevate unresolved issues to higher authority.
  • Prepare and submit yearly reports to Local and National Government.

Workplace Hazards – 5 Categories

Physical
Radiation, noise, temperature extremes, vibration, electric/magnetic fields. Acute: heat stroke, burns. Chronic: hearing loss, cancer.
Chemical
Solvents, pesticides, metals, gases, medications. Acute: irritation, chemical burns. Chronic: asthma, cancer, neurological disorders.
Biological
Viruses, bacteria, fungi, parasites (healthcare, lab, animal workers). Acute: flu, measles. Chronic: TB, HIV, HepB.
Mechanical / Ergonomic
Repetitive motion, poor workstation design, local vibration. Acute: strain. Chronic: carpal tunnel, back injury.
Psychosocial
Interpersonal conflict, shift work, sexual harassment, role conflict. Acute: fatigue, depression. Chronic: hypertension, alcoholism, mental illness.

OHN role: Anticipate, recognize, evaluate, and control hazards through surveillance, training, and engineering controls.

Levels of Prevention & Essential OHN Components

Primary Prevention
Pre‑employment exams, health education, PPE, engineering controls, vaccination.
Secondary Prevention
Screening & monitoring (audiometry, spirometry, lead levels), early detection, treatment.
Tertiary Prevention
Rehabilitation, job modification, return‑to‑work programs, chronic pain management.

Essential components of OHN practice:

  • Health promotion & prevention (primary, secondary, tertiary)
  • Worker & workplace hazard assessment / surveillance
  • Injury & illness investigation, analysis, prevention
  • Primary care (treatment, follow‑up, emergency)
  • Case management (coordinate care, cost‑effective)
  • Counseling (stress, lifestyle, psychosocial issues)
  • Management & administration
  • Legal/ethical monitoring
  • Research
  • Community orientation & resource linkage

Legal Bases & Workers' Compensation (ECC)

  • PD 626 – Employees' Compensation and State Insurance Fund (ECC).
  • Compensability: Injury must arise out of and in the course of employment; sickness must be an occupational disease.
  • Employer shall require pre‑employment and periodic medical examinations for exposed workers.
  • ECC can approve additional occupational diseases.
  • Sanitation Code (PD 856) Chapter VII – Industrial Hygiene.
  • RA 11058 – Occupational Safety and Health Standards (strengthened compliance).
  • DOH Occupational Health Program – under NCDPC, Environmental & Occupational Health Office (EOHO).

OHNAP: Occupational Health Nurses Association of the Philippines, Inc. – founded 1950 as Industrial Nursing Unit.

Public Health Nurse as Occupational Health Nurse

  • Workplace assessment: Conduct walk‑through surveys to identify hazards.
  • Worker health assessment: Pre‑placement, periodic, return‑to‑work exams; biological monitoring (blood lead, cholinesterase).
  • Health education: Train workers on PPE use, ergonomics, smoking cessation, stress management.
  • Surveillance: Monitor work‑related injuries and illnesses; report to DOLE/ECC.
  • Emergency response: First aid, medical evacuation, disaster preparedness in workplace.
  • Case management: Coordinate workers' compensation, rehabilitation, and return to work.
  • Advocacy: Promote healthy workplace policies, smoking bans, mental health support.
  • Coordination: Link with LGU, DOH, DOLE, and community resources.

Multidisciplinary team: OHN works with safety engineer, industrial hygienist, epidemiologist, ergonomist, occupational physician, and therapist.
